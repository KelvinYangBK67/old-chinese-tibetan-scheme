Old Chinese Tibetan Transcription Scheme

A scheme for spelling Old Chinese in Tibetan script, based on the Baxter–Sagart reconstruction and intended to be concise and natural. This scheme is primarily recreational.

Baxter–Sagart uvulars borrow the Tibetan palatal series. Abstract C, N and S indicate that the source does not identify a more specific consonant.

02

Minor syllables and preinitials

Minor syllables

Material before the Baxter–Sagart dot is written as a short preceding stack without a syllable delimiter. An explicit vowel takes its ordinary sign. A minor syllable with no written vowel takes ྄, keeping it distinct from explicit ə ྀ.

Tightly attached preinitials

A tightly attached preinitial joins the main initial in a single Tibetan stack. With two preinitials, the first stands before the stack and the second remains superscribed.

Pharyngealization, r and labialization

Pharyngealization ˤ, post-initial r and labialization ʷ use a fixed set of subjoined signs after the main initial.

P-rV and PrV both treat P as the main initial and place r beneath it. Thus *s-rut and *srut are both སྲུད. An initial rV still uses base ར.

Stop codas follow Tibetan suffix practice and use བ ད ག, rather than initial-position པ ཏ ཀ. Initial ʔ- uses ཨ, whereas final -ʔ uses འ.

06

Voiceless sonorants

The 2014 symbols m̥, n̥, ŋ̊, l̥ and r̥ receive no separate Tibetan letters. Each is written according to its later-established historical source as a preinitial plus an ordinary sonorant. An unresolved source uses unspecified C.
